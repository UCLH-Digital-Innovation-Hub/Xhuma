Purpose Statement

Primary Objective

To facilitate seamless, real-time access to patient clinical records strictly for the purpose of Direct Care.

The Problem

Clinicians require immediate access to primary care records (GP Connect) to make safe, informed treatment decisions. However, local clinical systems (like Epic) often speak different data languages (e.g., SOAP/C-CDA) than the modern national NHS APIs (FHIR).

The Solution: Xhuma

Xhuma acts as a highly secure, real-time translation layer. It securely connects to national NHS infrastructure (PDS and GP Connect), retrieves the data, and translates it into structured, localized formats that frontline clinicians can immediately view in their native systems.

Goal for Today

Obtain RIGG authorization to sign the National Data Sharing Arrangement (NDSA) for GP Connect.

The Clinical Experience: Care Everywhere

Leveraging Existing Interfaces

Care Everywhere is Epic's native interoperability interface, already actively used by clinicians to seamlessly share and view patient data between different Epic trusts.

Minimal Training Requirements

Because Care Everywhere is native Epic functionality, the training requirement for Xhuma is minimal. Any education required should simply be considered part of the wider Care Everywhere and standard Epic training offering. By translating GP Connect FHIR data into standard C-CDA documents, Xhuma effectively acts as a "virtual Epic site", securely piping primary care data directly into the exact interface clinicians already know and trust.

Architecture: Stateless Middleware

To understand how Xhuma operates securely, it is best described as a stateless middleware layer.

What is Middleware?

Middleware is the digital "glue" that sits between two otherwise incompatible systems. Local clinical systems speak different data languages than modern national NHS APIs. Instead of forcing them to talk directly, Xhuma sits in the middle, translates the requests in real-time, and securely passes the data back and forth.

What does Stateless mean?

Xhuma does not have a permanent memory. It does not store patient records, clinical data, or long-term state in a database. Every time a request comes in, Xhuma fetches the data from the NHS, translates it, delivers it to Epic, and immediately forgets it.

Key IG Benefit: This drastically reduces the Information Governance (IG) risk because there is no central database of primary care records to secure.

High-Level Security & Governance

Before diving into data flows, it is critical to highlight how Xhuma protects PHI:

  • No Persistent Storage: Xhuma is a transient middleware. Clinical data is processed in-memory and cached temporarily (with strict TTLs) purely for performance. No clinical records are persistently stored.
  • Network Security (mTLS): All inbound traffic is secured via Mutual TLS. Only explicitly allowlisted client certificates (validated by SHA-1 thumbprint) are accepted.
  • Authentication: Dual-layer security requiring both a secret API Key and a signed SAML Assertion detailing the requesting clinician's identity and role.
  • NHS API Assertions: All outbound requests to NHS APIs are secured using tightly scoped JSON Web Tokens (JWTs) signed with cryptographically secure RS512 keys.
  • PHI Redaction: Application logs are strictly scrubbed of PHI. NHS numbers are redacted from request URLs, and patient identifiers are never printed to console logs.

Observability & Monitoring

Xhuma operates with complete transparency. Our real-time monitoring suite tracks end-to-end latency, cache performance, and outbound NHS dependencies.

Safeguards Against Inappropriate Access

To address specific concerns regarding inappropriate staff access, Xhuma heavily relies on Epic's proven security model and extends it to the national spine:

Epic Safeguards & Break the Glass Accessing a GP record via Xhuma is protected by the exact same safeguards as any other Epic record. This includes strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) and Break the Glass protocols for sensitive records.
Bi-Directional Auditing When a clinician accesses a record, their Epic login, role, and physical location are cryptographically passed to the GP system. This ensures that both UCLH and the patient's home GP have a complete, transparent audit trail.
Hardcoded Purpose of Use The system strictly enforces the directcare purpose. It is impossible for a user to query the GP Connect API via Xhuma for research, secondary uses, or population health.

Governance & Legal Compliance

Xhuma is built strictly around NHS Information Governance (IG) frameworks to ensure absolute compliance:

National Data Opt-Out (NDOO) Compliance Xhuma seamlessly integrates with the national Spine. If a patient has registered a National Data Opt-Out, or if their PDS record flags data sharing restrictions, Xhuma automatically respects it. Data is never pulled against patient wishes.
Data Security and Protection Toolkit (DSPT) The architecture directly aligns with the 10 Data Security Standards. By ensuring transient, stateless data flows, we drastically reduce the attack surface and eliminate the need for complex, at-rest clinical data encryption policies.
Proactive Security Auditing The entire middleware layer is subjected to rigorous, independent Penetration Testing and automated SAST/DAST code scanning prior to deployment to ensure zero vulnerabilities.

Data Flow 1: Demographics (PDS)

Before any clinical data is requested, Xhuma verifies the patient's identity against the national Patient Demographics Service (PDS) using ITI-55.

Clinician Auditing When Epic binds the patient record, Xhuma captures the identity of the requesting clinician via an inbound SAML header. This identity is cryptographically passed through to the NHS infrastructure.

Alignment with NDSA Requirements

Xhuma's architecture is explicitly designed to comply with the GP Connect NDSA:

Strict Purpose of Use The system hardcodes the reason_for_request to directcare. It cannot be used for secondary uses, research, or population health management.
Data Minimization The requested_scope is strictly limited to patient/*.read. We only pull what is necessary for the clinician to treat the patient in front of them.
End-to-End Auditability Every request includes the clinician's unique identifier, role, and organization (passed via SAML). Xhuma implements OpenTelemetry tracing and comprehensive audit logging to ensure every transaction is fully traceable.

Appendix: DPIA Summary (1/2)

Key considerations aligned with NHSE Data Protection Impact Assessment requirements:

1. Nature of Processing Xhuma retrieves clinical data via secure, real-time NHS APIs. Data is processed entirely in-memory and translated into C-CDA format. There is absolutely no persistent storage or long-term caching of the medical record.
2. Scope of Processing Processing is limited strictly to individual patients presenting for care at UCLH. The data retrieved is rigorously scoped to structured GP Connect records (allergies, medications, immunisations).
3. Purpose of Processing To provide treating clinicians with immediate, accurate primary care records to ensure patient safety, prevent adverse drug reactions, and reduce unnecessary duplicate diagnostic testing.

Appendix: DPIA Summary (2/2)

4. Necessity and Proportionality Alternative methods (e.g., calling GP practices, requesting faxes) are slow, error-prone, and introduce significant clinical risk during out-of-hours care. Automated API retrieval via Xhuma is the most secure and proportionate method available.
5. Identifying and Mitigating Risk The primary IG risk is unauthorized access to a national record. This is mitigated through Epic's strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC), mandatory Break-the-Glass protocols, and cryptographic bi-directional auditing back to the patient's GP.
6. Legal Basis for Processing Processing is conducted exclusively under the legal basis of Direct Care (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e) 'public task' and Article 9(2)(h) 'provision of health or social care').
